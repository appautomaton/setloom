# Expressive Electronic Arrangement

Ground decisions in the brief, actual material and scoped listening feedback.
Translate impressions into production choices without requiring the user to
supply technical vocabulary.

- Identify the musical character to preserve and the effect to develop before
  choosing an operation. A theme suggests associations, not a key or preset.
- Compose notes, articulation, timbre and spatial response together. A sound can
  generate the idea; the arrangement can change what its instrument needs to do.
- Let musical function and independent behavior determine part boundaries.
  Register and track labels do not establish a role or a required layer count.
- Develop recognizable gestures through tension, return and space across the
  form. Revise interacting parts together when useful; evaluate their ensemble.

Read only what the current question needs:

- [Musical revision](references/musical-revision.md): interpreting feedback,
  developing relationships and evaluating the complete arrangement.
- [Sound, gesture and control](references/expressive-controls.md): designing
  instruments, articulation and effects, or translating expressive behavior.
- [Reference reconstruction](../setloom-reference-reconstruction/SKILL.md):
  recovering or checking musical information against a recording.

Use available modalities to challenge the musical explanation, including where
it fails. Work patiently through internal passes, then deliver the commissioned
audio scope. Keep notes, controls, patches, routing and effect automation editable;
keep each song's decisions and parameters with that song.
