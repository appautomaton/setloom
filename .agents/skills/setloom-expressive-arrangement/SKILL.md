# Expressive Electronic Arrangement

Exercise compositional and production judgment. A supplied sound can communicate
intent without a technical description; discovering what makes it work belongs
to the agent.

## Musical priorities

- Compose relationships across the phrase and form: foreground, propulsion,
  harmonic tension, response and space. Layers should interact and develop;
  density, doubling and louder processing do not establish depth.
- Preserve the intended identity of the lead while choosing complementary
  voicings, rhythms, registers and entrances. Restraint and withdrawal are
  compositional resources. Genre labels do not supply the arrangement.
- Treat movement within a sound as musical material. Pitch, articulation, colour
  and spatial behavior may need independent control. Choose a representation and
  instrument architecture that preserve their relationship.
- Divide tracks by musical independence, not separator labels. Distinguish an
  independent voice from a continuing carrier, its modulation and its returns;
  overlapping evidence must not become duplicate performances.

Use multimodal evidence to resolve concrete musical uncertainties. Inspect the
actual source and rendered result; numerical summaries alone can conceal the
problem. Work patiently through phrases and repeats, judge changes in ensemble,
then complete the commissioned scope. Automate understood operations without
outsourcing the musical explanation to a pipeline. Revise weak decisions before
asking the human to evaluate the result.

Read [Gesture and control](references/expressive-controls.md) when translating
reference motion or when the note scaffold survives but the musical effect does
not. Use the adjacent [reconstruction skill](../setloom-reference-reconstruction/SKILL.md)
for faithful recovery of source parts. Keep the performance, control mappings,
patches and routing editable; deliver music for listening.
