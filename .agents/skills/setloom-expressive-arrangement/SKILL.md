# Expressive Electronic Arrangement

Ground decisions in the brief, actual material and scoped listening feedback.
Translate impressions into production choices without requiring technical
vocabulary from the user.

- Identify the character to preserve and the effect to develop before choosing
  an operation. A theme suggests associations, not a key or preset.
- Compose notes, articulation, timbre and spatial response together; a sound can
  generate the idea. Include bass and percussion character; equal pitches and
  onsets can produce different grooves.
- Let musical function and independent behavior determine part boundaries.
  Register and track labels do not establish a role or a required layer count.
- Develop recognizable gestures through tension, return and space across the
  form. Revise interacting parts together when useful; evaluate their ensemble.

Read only what the current question needs:

- [Musical revision](references/musical-revision.md): interpret feedback, locate
  causes in the score, instrument or mix, and evaluate the full arrangement.
- [Sound, gesture and control](references/expressive-controls.md): design
  instruments, articulation and effects, or translate expressive behavior.
- [Reference reconstruction](../setloom-reference-reconstruction/SKILL.md): when
  missing or ambiguous source information requires investigation.

Keep notes, controls, patches, routing and effect automation editable in the
song's source. Deliver the commissioned audio scope.
