# Sound, gesture and control

## Choose the musical role

Identify what a sound contributes and how that contribution changes through the
phrase. Register alone does not establish lead, foundation or atmosphere.

Separate musical voices when their behavior warrants it, and render layers when
independent control improves the sound. A continuing carrier, its moving texture
and its returns need not be independent played parts. Avoid duplicating their
contribution in the mix. For uncertain source assignments, use
[reference evidence](../../../../docs/reconstruction.md#representation-and-provenance).

## Design the whole gesture

Choose excitation, harmonic structure, envelopes and modulation for the intended
character. Articulation can distinguish an arrival, continuation or reply even
when pitch and onset stay unchanged. Relate internal pulses and decay to the
actual groove; deliberate repetition need not be randomized.

Consider onset, sustained motion and release together. Changing one may alter the
apparent rhythm or mask another part. A supported score can need a new instrument
response rather than new notes.

For bass, preserve the relationship between low-frequency weight, upper texture,
pitch movement and pulse. Independent control of foundation and texture can help,
but is not a required layer architecture. For percussion, attend to strike weight,
pitch sweep, transient/body balance, decay, brightness and space as the material
requires. Check accents, continuations and fills in relation to the bass and
phrase. Velocity variation alone does not establish character or groove.

## Compose the space

An effect can answer a line, prolong tension, change perceived distance or carry
a transition. Its pitch content, rhythm and decay determine that role. Judge the
dry gesture and returns separately and together, including how the next phrase
meets their tails.

Shape sends and returns where development calls for it. Filtering, ducking and
stereo motion can clarify a response or erase its identity. Check the rendered
relationship in context; processing amounts do not establish depth or presence.

## Translate and verify behavior

Measured band envelopes describe activity, not original synthesis parameters.
Map them to the new instrument's actual response. Check the combined path through
velocity, expression, envelopes, gain and effects: multiplying plausible controls
can suppress notes twice, amplify leakage or flatten accents. Smoothing and
nonlinear mappings also change articulation. Constant velocity can coexist with
expressive motion in other controls; verify that the renderer uses those controls.

Develop accents, harmonic turns, colour and responses within the current phrases.
Measured curves, authored envelopes and low-frequency oscillators are available
means. Keep their provenance, controller semantics, units and routing with the
patch. Use independent sound sources for new performances; distinguish any
authorized retained reference audio.
